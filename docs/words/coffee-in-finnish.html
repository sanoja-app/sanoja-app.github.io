"Coffee" in Finnish: kahvi

kahvi
Say it: KAH-hvi. In Finnish, stress is always on the first syllable.

Kahvi means "coffee". To offer or ask for some, you use the partitive: Haluatko kahvia? ("Would you like some coffee?")

Why kahvia?

After verbs like haluta ("to want") and juoda ("to drink"), Finnish uses the partitive form for an unspecified amount: kahvi becomes kahvia. So you say Juon kahvia ("I'm drinking coffee") and Haluatko kahvia? ("Would you like some coffee?").

Finland is regularly listed among the countries that drink the most coffee per person, and a kahvitauko is an established part of the working day.

Example sentences

  • Haluatko kahvia?Would you like some coffee?
  • Juon kahvia joka aamu.I drink coffee every morning.
  • Kahvi, kiitos.A coffee, please.
  • Pidetään kahvitauko.Let's take a coffee break.
